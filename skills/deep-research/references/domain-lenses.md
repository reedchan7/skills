# Domain lenses

A lens tells the protocol where the owners of facts live in a field, what
counts as authority there, how fast facts expire, the traps that catch
generalists, and the one or two verification moves specific to the field.
Pick one primary lens in Phase 0 and add a second when the question straddles
fields (a pricing question about a regulated medical device is market +
medical + legal). The protocol itself never changes; the lens fills in its
blanks. Read only the rows you chose (`rg '^\| \*\*Technology' domain-lenses.md`
pulls one) plus the cross-cutting section when the topic touches China.

| Lens | Owners of the fact (tier A) | Authority signals | Recency | Traps | Field-specific checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Technology and software** | source code at the pinned version; official docs and changelogs; the spec or RFC; the issue tracker; release notes; benchmarks with published harness | maintainer or standards body; reproducible numbers; version stated | rapid: behaviour is version-bound; anything undated or older than the current major is "as of" | tutorials describing old versions; SEO listicles; vendor benchmarks without a harness; confusing announced with shipped | pin the version in every claim; read the code or docs for the exact option; check issue tracker for "known broken"; for popularity use dated download or star trends, never a single count |
| **Academic and scientific** | the peer-reviewed paper (and its data or code); systematic reviews and meta-analyses; preprints labelled as such; registries (trials, protocols) | peer review, replication, sample size and design, pre-registration; citation counts only as a discovery aid | moderate to slow; check for retraction or a later replication before citing anything older than five years | citing the abstract for a claim the body qualifies; press releases inflating effect sizes; preprint treated as settled; a single study treated as consensus; prestige or h-index as proof | resolve the DOI and confirm title and authors match (a resolving DOI with the wrong title is a fabrication pattern); read the methods and the limitations paragraph; distinguish "not reported" from "no effect"; grade the body of evidence, not the loudest paper |
| **Market, company, and competitive** | audited filings (10-K, annual report, prospectus); regulator databases; the company's own dated disclosures; primary datasets from statistical agencies; studies with published method | proximity to the event: regulator → first party → transparent study → analyst → news | rapid: figures carry period and unit ("FY2025 revenue, USD"); anything without a period is a rumour | TAM/SAM/SOM conflation; private-company "revenue" from press estimates; the same press release relayed as ten sources; vendor-sponsored analyst reports; funding round size confused with valuation | separate public (filed) from reported (press-cited) figures and label each; every metric with period and unit; two independent figures within 10% corroborate, otherwise show both with the cause; check whether the "analyst" was paid |
| **Product and user experience** | the product itself (dated screenshots or hands-on); official pricing and docs pages; changelogs; app-store and review aggregates with counts; the vendor's issue tracker | first-party artefacts and large-n user signal; single reviews are tier C colour | rapid: pricing and features change without notice; date every observation | reviews planted or incentivised; "best X" affiliate pages; feature lists copied from marketing | one-star reviews reveal unmet needs, five-star what to preserve; plugin ecosystems and migration guides show what the core lacks; upvoted issues are demand signals; archive the pricing page when citing a price |
| **Legal and regulatory** | the statute, regulation, ruling, docket, or official guidance at the relevant date and jurisdiction; official gazettes and registers | primacy of the primary text; court level; official status (in force, proposed, repealed) | mixed: text is stable, status changes; always state jurisdiction and as-of date | commentary cited as law; the wrong jurisdiction; a bill treated as enacted; dicta treated as holding; model knowledge of "current law" that is stale | quote the operative text and confirm it supports the proposition as stated; check currency (amendments, appeals, superseding rules) through the official source; tag everything not retrieved as `[model knowledge — verify]` and say a lawyer must confirm |
| **Medical and health** | systematic reviews and guidelines from recognised bodies; RCTs and registries; regulator labels and approvals; official epidemiology | evidence hierarchy with GRADE-style up and down grading; guideline body and date | moderate; guidelines are revised, approvals change; check the guideline's date and the regulator's current label | surrogate endpoints read as outcomes; relative risk without absolute risk; animal or in-vitro results generalised to humans; supplement marketing | report absolute effects with the population and time point; state the study design behind each claim; note conflicts of interest and funding; flag that this is information, not clinical advice |
| **Finance and investment** | filings and audited statements; exchange and regulator data; central-bank and statistical releases; fund documents | audited > reported > estimated; official releases over commentary | rapid; every number carries its date and currency | survivorship in performance claims; nominal versus real; unlabeled fiscal versus calendar periods; forecasts quoted as facts | label forecasts as the forecaster's; convert and date currencies; reconcile figures across two filings; flag any advice-shaped conclusion as not advice |
| **Policy, society, and history** | primary documents and archives; official statistics; legislation and hearings; contemporaneous records; peer-reviewed history and social science | archival provenance; representativeness of data; methodological transparency | slow for events, moderate for interpretation; statistics have revision cycles | presentism; single-narrative sources; polls without method; quotations that were never said; Wikipedia cited instead of its references | trace quotations to the original speech or document; state the poll's method, n, and dates; present competing interpretations with their evidence bases; distinguish what happened from why |
| **People and organisations (due diligence)** | official registries and filings; the person's or organisation's own statements; court and regulator records; reputable dated journalism with named reporters | official records first, then first-party, then named journalism | mixed; roles and status change; date every fact | name collisions (two people, one name); social profiles taken at face value; rumours relayed as findings; using the requester's private knowledge to "discover" what they already know | disambiguate identity before anything else (role, employer, location, dates); separate verified record from claim; record what could not be found per channel and say "not found in <channels>"; respect privacy: report what public records show, nothing inferred about protected characteristics |
| **Recent developments and public sentiment** | dated primary announcements; the original post, thread, or talk; changelogs and release notes; large-n discussion threads read as signal, not fact | primacy and date; engagement is reach, not truth | days to weeks; query with day-level dates; state the window searched | treating discussion volume as evidence of fact; a single viral post as consensus; missing non-English platforms; recycled news | quote the primary announcement, not the coverage; report sentiment with counts and platforms; tag community claims as tier C and corroborate any fact drawn from them |

## Cross-cutting lens: Chinese-language ecosystem

When the topic, the market, or the user is Chinese, the English index misses
most of the evidence. Add these owners to whichever lens applies:

- **Official**: 国家统计局, 部委官网与公告, 中国政府网, 国家企业信用信息公示系统, 证监会 · 上交所 · 深交所 · 港交所披露易 (公告与年报), 国家知识产权局, 国家药监局 (NMPA), 网信办与工信部备案与公示.
- **Academic**: CNKI 知网, 万方, CSCD; universities' repositories; Chinese preprint servers; Chinese-language versions of international papers.
- **Technology**: GitHub with Chinese READMEs, Gitee, 官方文档中文版, 掘金, 知乎 (专栏 as tier B when authored and cited, 回答 as tier C), CSDN and 博客园 (tier C unless the author is identifiable and cites), V2EX, SegmentFault, 阿里云 · 腾讯云 · 华为云 developer communities, 公众号 articles (date and author required).
- **Market and product**: 公司公告 and 年报, 艾瑞 · QuestMobile · 易观 (paid estimates: label as such), 36氪 · 晚点 · 界面 · 财新 (named journalism), 小红书 · 微博 · 抖音 · B站 (sentiment, tier C), 应用商店 and 七麦 data.
- **Searching**: search in Chinese with the field's own vocabulary (英文术语 and 中文术语 are different queries); when a general search engine is the only tool, add the platform name to the query (`知乎`, `公告`, `年报`); expect paywalls and JS-only pages and record them as failed channels rather than silently skipping.
- **Traps**: 某专家表示 / 据统计 / 业内人士 without a name and a source are rejected; 软文 (paid placement) reads like journalism; screenshots of announcements are not the announcement; simplified and traditional variants and romanisations are separate searches.

## Using a lens

In Phase 0 the lens is recorded in plan.md. In Phase 2 its owners become the
"where to look first" of every worker brief and the `site:` narrowing of the
lead's own searches; its recency rule sets the currency window in the ledger;
its traps are checked once in the counter-read. A lens that turns out to be
wrong is changed in plan.md with a one-line reason, not silently.
